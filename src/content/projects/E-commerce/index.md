## Levels of E-Commerce Businesses in South Africa

This document outlines the progression of e-commerce business complexity, from informal online selling to fully custom enterprise platforms. Each level introduces new technical, operational, and regulatory challenges, along with solutions commonly used in South Africa.

| Level | Model                           | Description                                                                 | Common Challenges (SA Context)                                              | Typical Solutions                                                                       |
| ----- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | Social Commerce                 | Selling through WhatsApp, Facebook Marketplace, Instagram, or Telegram      | Limited reach, manual order tracking, payment trust issues                  | Payment links (SnapScan, Zapper), simple spreadsheets for orders, customer testimonials |
| **2** | Online Marketplace Seller       | Selling through established platforms like Takealot Marketplace or Bob Shop | Marketplace fees reduce margins, high competition, limited branding control | Optimized product listings, competitive pricing, strong product photography and reviews |
| **3** | Basic Website Store             | Template-based store built with Shopify, WooCommerce, Wix or Squarespace    | Plugin conflicts, security risks, payment gateway setup                     | Managed hosting, SSL certificates, trusted payment gateways like PayFast or Ozow        |
| **4** | Integrated E-Commerce Platform  | Store integrated with inventory, delivery services, and accounting tools    | Integration complexity, logistics coordination, scaling customer support    | Courier APIs (Aramex, The Courier Guy), automation tools like Zapier, helpdesk systems  |
| **5** | Multi-Vendor Platform           | A marketplace where other vendors can sell products through your platform   | Vendor management, dispute resolution, commission tracking                  | Vendor dashboards, automated payouts, vendor contracts and service agreements           |
| **6** | Custom E-Commerce Platform      | Fully custom web platform built by a development team                       | High development costs, infrastructure complexity, maintenance requirements | Cloud infrastructure, modular architecture, CI/CD pipelines, dedicated developers       |
| **7** | Enterprise Omnichannel Commerce | Large-scale platforms integrated with ERP, CRM, POS, and logistics systems  | System synchronization, data compliance (POPIA), high traffic performance   | API-driven architecture, load balancing, distributed databases, data governance         |

### South African E-Commerce Context

#### Common Challenges

- Payments, Many customers still prefer EFT or cash alternatives due to trust issues with online payments.
- Logistics
- Delivery infrastructure can be inconsistent, especially in rural or informal areas.
- Trust and Fraud
- Consumers often require social proof, reviews, and recognizable payment gateways.
- Connectivity
- Mobile users dominate online shopping in South Africa, but data costs and network reliability affect browsing behavior.
  
| Payment Provider | Description                                                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **PayFast**      | One of the most widely used South African payment gateways         |
| **PayGate**      | Enterprise-grade gateway supporting cards and alternative payments |
| **Ozow**         | Instant EFT payments directly from bank accounts                   |
| **SnapScan**     | QR-based payments popular for small businesses                     |
| **Zapper**       | QR payment system commonly used by retailers and restaurants       |
